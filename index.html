Reading it

  • Narrow beats wide. Drag scales with width, thrust does not — halving the width is worth roughly 1.4× the speed at the same thruster count.
  • Thrusters stack in series. There is no top speed; adding thrusters adds thrust without adding drag, so long-and-narrow is the fast shape.
  • These are v0. A leg runs about 10 km/s below this before its midpoint and 10 above after, so a ship reads as two plateaus 20 apart.
  • Weight is 200 × tiles + 40,000, and machines are massless — only foundation counts. The grid fixes weight at 200,000; a heavier hull is proportionally slower to accelerate but reaches the same cruise.

Where the numbers come from

Cruise is the speed at which thrust balances drag, solved from the game's own acceleration expression:

(thrust / (1 + weight / 10000000) - ((1500 * speed * speed + 1500 * abs(speed)) * (width * 0.5) + 10000) * sign(speed)) / weight / 60

Thrust per thruster is not exposed by the API. It was measured across ship classes at the fleet's fill setpoint, and the page recomputes each measured ship from the resulting constant as a self-check — the build fails if any prediction drifts more than 1%.

Caveats

  • Measured at the fleet's fill setpoint (P=43, thruster fluid ~426-451), NOT physical maxima. Values are v0: subtract 10 before a leg's midpoint, add 10 after.
  • Validated against a 10-wide hull with 2 legendary thrusters at weight 168,000: predicted v0 391 against measured plateaus of 381.3 and 401.
  • platform.speed is tiles/tick; km/s = speed × 60, where 60 is the design UPS and 1 tile = 1 km.
